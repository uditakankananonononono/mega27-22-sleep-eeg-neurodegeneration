ISEF judge critique — Project 22 DREAMING (pre-data stage)
Overall judge assessment

Current status: a promising research question, but not yet a research contribution.

At this stage, I would not score the project on the ambition of “early neurodegeneration biomarker discovery.” I would score the research design. The central risk is that this becomes a large feature-screening exercise on a noisy clinical prediction dataset, producing a statistically significant-looking EEG pattern that is actually a proxy for age, hospital, sleep-disorder burden, recording year, or label construction.

The project becomes ISEF-competitive only if it can demonstrate:

A genuinely interpretable biological hypothesis before modeling

Strict prevention of leakage/confounding

External validation

A mechanistic interpretation beyond “feature importance says X matters”

The PhysioNet 2026 Challenge is relevant because it is explicitly built around PSG prediction of future cognitive impairment using multicenter data and hidden-site evaluation. 
PhysioNet
+1
 However, participating in a challenge alone would not constitute a novel scientific discovery; the project needs a biological insight beyond winning a prediction benchmark.

Major weakness #1: “Biomarker discovery” is currently underspecified

Right now:

"Find a non-invasive interpretable sleep-EEG candidate biomarker"

is a goal, not a hypothesis.

A judge will ask:

What biological phenomenon are you testing?

Examples of stronger hypotheses:

"Early neurodegeneration disrupts REM-NREM transition dynamics because hippocampal-thalamocortical regulation deteriorates."

"Reduced NREM slow-wave organization reflects impaired glymphatic/cortical synchronization before diagnosed cognitive impairment."

"Altered spindle–slow oscillation coupling indicates early disruption of memory consolidation circuits."

Then your features become tests of biology, not a fishing expedition.

Current feature list:

spectral power

entropy

connectivity

coupling

transitions

REM physiology

is essentially "everything measurable from EEG."

A skeptical judge will ask:

"If 300 EEG features are tested, why should I believe the winning feature reflects neurodegeneration rather than multiple-comparison luck?"

Major weakness #2: The outcome label is not equivalent to early Alzheimer's biology

This is probably the largest scientific issue.

A future ICD-coded MCI/AD/dementia diagnosis is a clinical administrative endpoint, not a pure neurodegeneration measurement.

It mixes:

Alzheimer's disease

vascular cognitive impairment

Lewy body disease

diagnostic practice differences

healthcare access

physician coding behavior

Therefore:

A sleep EEG predictor may discover:

"Who later receives a cognitive impairment diagnosis"

rather than:

"Who has early Alzheimer's pathology."

Those are related but not identical.

Your wording must probably change from:

"early neurodegeneration biomarker"

to something more defensible:

"sleep EEG signatures associated with future clinical cognitive impairment risk."

You can discuss neurodegeneration as a hypothesis afterward.

Major weakness #3: Hidden confounding is the existential threat

A model can easily cheat.

Potential confounders:

Age

Age is the strongest predictor of dementia risk and strongly affects sleep EEG.

A model may simply learn:

"older brains have different sleep."

You need age-adjusted analyses.

Hospital/site effects

Different hospitals may have:

different EEG machines

different montages

different preprocessing

different patient populations

The PhysioNet challenge specifically emphasizes multicenter PSG heterogeneity and hidden-site evaluation because generalization is difficult. 
George B. Moody PhysioNet Challenge

A feature that works in one hospital but fails elsewhere is not a biomarker.

Sleep disorders

Obstructive sleep apnea, medications, depression, obesity, cardiovascular disease, and insomnia can alter sleep EEG.

You need to determine:

Is your signal:

A) neurodegeneration-related?

or

B) a general marker of unhealthy sleep?

Major weakness #4: "Interpretable AI" can become post-hoc storytelling

A common ISEF failure mode:

Train model

Find important feature

Explain biological meaning afterward

Judges are skeptical of this.

Better:

Pre-register candidate mechanisms.

Example:

Primary hypothesis:

NREM delta organization predicts future impairment.

Secondary:

REM instability.

spindle coupling.

entropy.

Then test those.

The model should validate your hypothesis, not create one after seeing results.

Major weakness #5: Benchmark beating is dangerous framing

"Beat benchmark" is not necessarily a scientific contribution.

A challenge leaderboard rewards prediction.

ISEF rewards:

novelty

rigor

insight

A model achieving AUROC improvement by adding 500 features is less impressive than a simpler model showing:

"A two-feature REM-transition abnormality predicts future cognitive impairment across hospitals."

The second is closer to a biomarker.

What I would redirect the project toward
Option 1 (strongest): Mechanistic sleep architecture biomarker

Question:

Can specific sleep-state transition dynamics predict future cognitive decline independently of age and sleep-disorder burden?

Focus:

REM latency

REM fragmentation

N2→N3 transitions

N3 stability

spindle/slow oscillation coupling

Why stronger:

A biological story exists.

Option 2: "Minimal biomarker" challenge

Instead of maximizing prediction:

Question:

What is the smallest interpretable EEG feature set that retains predictive ability?

Compare:

age only

age + clinical variables

age + sleep architecture

age + EEG biomarker

Success:

A compact signature.

Option 3: Cross-dataset replication

Discovery:

Dataset A.

Validation:

Dataset B.

No tuning on validation.

This is probably the highest-value addition.

Minimum viable ISEF-grade pipeline

I would require:

Phase 1 — Biological hypothesis

Predefine 5–10 candidate features.

Phase 2 — Baseline models

Must include:

age only

demographics only

age + sleep variables

EEG biomarker model

Otherwise improvement cannot be interpreted.

Phase 3 — Leakage controls

Must test:

site-held-out validation

age adjustment

recording-year effects

sleep disorder adjustment

Phase 4 — Interpretation

Not:

"SHAP says delta power."

Instead:

"Reduced N3 organization is associated with increased risk after controlling for age/site and aligns with known sleep-dependent memory physiology."

The difference between an average ML biomarker project and a strong ISEF project will not be the neural network. It will be whether you can prove:

"This EEG signature reflects an interpretable biological change associated with future cognitive decline, not merely who was older, sicker, or recorded at a different hospital."

That is the scientific question judges will care about.
