ISEF reviewer verdict (Round 2): DREAMING 22

Current status: promising hypothesis + careful engineering scaffold, not yet a biomarker study.

The strongest part is the discipline: SHA256 verification, outcome-blind preprocessing, no bridging of unknown gaps, and explicit admission that the pilot cannot satisfy the REM-event threshold. The biggest danger is that the project currently risks becoming a beautiful signal-processing exercise without a demonstrated biological question.

A grand-award-level computational biology project would need the chain:

sleep physiology hypothesis → reproducible EEG feature → same-task benchmark → prospective cognitive association → independent replication

Right now the chain stops at:

sleep physiology hypothesis → reproducible EEG feature (not yet demonstrated across people).

1. Most damaging weaknesses
Weakness 1 — The biological claim is ahead of the data

The current feature:

median log(target/source) bandpower during direct N2→REM transitions

is interesting, but the biological interpretation is unclear.

Questions a judge will immediately ask:

Why should a transition-specific spectral ratio represent future cognitive decline?

Why N2→REM rather than N3→N2, REM onset, spindle events, cyclic alternating patterns, or slow oscillation coupling?

Is the feature measuring:

thalamocortical transition dynamics?

arousal instability?

electrode/reference artifact?

normal inter-individual sleep architecture variation?

Without a mechanistic bridge, the novelty claim is vulnerable.

Falsifier: If the feature cannot be separated from simpler sleep variables (transition duration, REM percentage, age-associated architecture variables), the biological interpretation weakens.

Weakness 2 — The pilot event count exposes a fundamental unit-of-analysis problem

You have:

2 N2→REM events

29 N2→N3 events

This is not just a sample-size problem. It questions whether the proposed event is the correct biological unit.

A judge will ask:

"If the phenomenon is biologically meaningful, why is it so rare that a single public PSG provides only two observations?"

You cannot solve this by lowering the threshold until it works. That looks like p-hacking.

The correct response is to change the analysis unit before seeing outcomes.

Weakness 3 — No baseline comparison yet

The project explicitly needs:

fair same-task benchmark beat

Currently there is no benchmark.

A reviewer will expect comparison against at least:

Sleep-architecture baseline

Examples:

total N3 percentage

REM percentage

sleep efficiency

N2 duration

transition counts

Spectral baseline

Examples:

N3 delta power

gamma power

multiband EEG summary

Because prior work such as SOF already reported combinations of N3/gamma/multivariate features, simply adding another spectral ratio may be judged incremental.

Weakness 4 — Novelty risk against prior literature

The vulnerable statement is:

"new biological insight"

A skeptical reviewer will say:

"Sleep EEG biomarkers of cognitive decline already exist. What specifically is new?"

Prior art includes:

N3-related measures

gamma activity

multivariate EEG features

brain-age-index approaches

Therefore novelty cannot be:

"EEG predicts cognition"

That is already established.

The novelty must become something like:

"a previously untested transition-dynamics feature describing state-change physiology."

That is narrower and defensible.

3. What NOT to do

Avoid:

lowering REM event threshold from 3 → 2 because the pilot failed

claiming Alzheimer's prediction from Sleep-EDF

Your 20 engineering tests demonstrate:

software correctness

They do not demonstrate:

biological validity.
